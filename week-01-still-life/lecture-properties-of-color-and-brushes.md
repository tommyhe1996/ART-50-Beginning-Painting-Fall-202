# ART 50 – Week 01 Lecture Notes
## Painting Process: The Properties of Color, Paint Mediums, and Brushes

---

# 1. The Properties of Color

## Hue

**Hue = the name of the color.**

Examples:
- Red
- Yellow
- Blue
- Green
- Orange
- Violet

The lecture references:
- Itten's Color Wheel
- Munsell's Color Wheel

---

## Value

**Value = the lightness or darkness of a hue.**

The lecture uses Cézanne's still life painting to show how color values can be understood.

A grayscale version of the painting helps reveal the different light and dark values.

### Important idea

Even when colors are different, they can have similar or different values.

Value will be especially important when painting:

- highlights
- midtones
- shadows
- three-dimensional form

---

## Intensity

**Intensity = the brightness of a color.**

The lecture compares:

- Low-intensity color
- High-intensity color

### Low Intensity

Colors appear more muted or less bright.

### High Intensity

Colors appear brighter and stronger.

---

# 2. Local Color

**Local color = the natural color of an object in ordinary daylight.**

Example:

If an apple naturally appears red in ordinary daylight, red is its local color.

---

# 3. Arbitrary Color

**Arbitrary color has no realistic or natural relationship to the object being depicted.**

It can be used for:

- emotional meaning
- expressive meaning
- creative interpretation

Example:

A tree does not need to be painted green.

It could be painted:

- purple
- orange
- blue
- pink

if the artist chooses the color for expressive reasons.

---

# 4. Making Grays

For beginning painting students, the lecture recommends using:

**Chromatic grays**

These are grays created through color relationships rather than relying only on black and white.

---

# 5. Paint Mediums

## Oil Paint Mediums

### Linseed Oil

Functions:

- Increases flow
- Increases transparency
- Increases gloss

---

### Stand Oil

A thick linseed oil.

Functions:

- Excellent leveling properties
- Increases high gloss
- Slower drying
- Creates a smooth film
- Creates a durable film
- Creates a flexible film

---

### Turpentine

Functions as a:

**Thinner**

The lecture notes that when turpentine is mixed with linseed oil half-and-half, it can become a basic all-purpose oil painting medium.

---

### Gamsol

Functions as:

- Oil paint medium
- Thinner
- Cleaner

It is described in the lecture as non-flammable.

---

### Citrus Thinner

Functions as:

- Thinner
- Solvent for oil paint

---

---

## Matte

# 7. Paint Brushes

## Badger Hair

### Description

Traditionally used for blending oil paint on canvas.

Characteristics:

- Thickest near the point
- Relatively thin near the root
- Bushy appearance

### Media

- Oil

---

## Camel Hair

Camel-hair brushes do not actually come from camels.

They may be made from:

- squirrel
- goat
- ox
- pony
- blends of several hairs

### Uses

Commonly used for:

- watercolor
- lettering

### Media

- Lettering
- Tempera
- Watercolor

---

## Hog Bristle

Characteristics:

- Natural curve
- V-shaped split or "flag" at the tip
- Strong
- Suitable for medium to thick paint
- Can spread paint smoothly and evenly

The lecture recommends pure hog-bristle brushes as a useful option for both oil and acrylic painting.

### Media

- Acrylic
- Oil

---

## Kevrin / Mongoose Hair

Characteristics:

- Strong
- Resilient
- Long-wearing
- Medium to professional quality

### Media

- Acrylic
- Oil

---

## Kolinsky Sable

Kolinsky Sable does not actually come from a sable.

It comes from the tail of a species of mink in the weasel family.

Characteristics:

- Strong
- Good spring
- Good "snap"
- Retains its shape
- Holds a very fine point or edge
- Professional quality
- Can last many years with proper care

### Media

- Oil
- Watercolor

---

## Ox Hair

Characteristics:

- Strong body
- Silken texture
- Resilient
- Good snap
- Does not have a very fine tip

Useful for:

- Wash brushes
- Flat brushes

### Media

- Lettering
- Watercolor

---

## Pony Hair

Characteristics:

- Soft
- Strong
- Often used for scholastic brushes
- Can be blended with other hairs

### Media

- Acrylic
- Scholastic
- Tempera
- Watercolor

---

## Red Sable

Red Sable comes from members of the weasel family with reddish hair.

Characteristics:

- Can perform similarly to Kolinsky
- More economical than Kolinsky
- Can be blended with ox hair

### Media

- Oil
- Watercolor

---

## Sabeline

Sabeline is:

**Light-colored ox hair dyed to resemble red sable.**

Often used in:

- lettering brushes
- watercolor brushes

It may be mixed with sable to reduce cost.

### Media

- Lettering
- Watercolor

---

## Squirrel Hair

Characteristics:

- Very fine
- Thin
- Points well
- Has little "snap"
- Works well with liquid paint and ink

### Media

- Lettering
- Watercolor

---

## Synthetic Brushes

Synthetic brushes are made from:

- Nylon
- Polyester

A common synthetic filament is:

**Taklon**

### Advantages

1. Less prone to damage from solvents, insects, and paints.
2. Easier to clean than animal-hair brushes.
3. Durable and less prone to breakage.
4. Well suited for acrylic painting.

### Important for ART 50

Synthetic brushes can be especially practical when working with acrylic paint.

# My Week 01 Learning Notes

## Most Important Ideas

- Hue, value, and intensity are three different properties of color.
- Value helps describe light and shadow.
- Intensity describes how bright or muted a color appears.
- Local color is realistic.
- Arbitrary color is expressive rather than realistic.
- Different painting mediums change how paint behaves.
- Different brush materials are designed for different kinds of paint.
- Synthetic and hog-bristle brushes can both be useful for acrylic painting.

## What I Need to Apply

For my still life, I should focus on:

1. Choosing one monochromatic hue.
2. Establishing a clear light source.
3. Keeping track of value.
4. Building the painting from base color toward shading.
5. Choosing brushes that work well with acrylic paint.
