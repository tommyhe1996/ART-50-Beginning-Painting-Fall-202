# Part 3 – Creating My Still Life

## Setup

- Receive the ping-pong balls and dish from the instructor.
- Arrange them into a still-life setup.
- Observe the setup and select an interesting viewing angle.
- Do **not** look straight down from above.
- Take a photograph of the setup for future reference.

---

## Composition

Use the entire canvas as part of the composition.

### Composition Steps

1. Observe the setup.
2. Identify:
   - top
   - bottom
   - left
   - right
3. Mark these locations on the canvas.
4. Draw the dish and balls.
5. Add an additional ball from imagination.
6. Think about visual balance.
7. Develop the background at the same time as the objects.

---

## Background

The background should be planned from the beginning.

Do not:

- paint all of the balls and dish first
- leave the background completely empty until the end

The background can come from:

- the actual scene
- photographs
- imagination

---

## Perspective and Proportion

Show depth by changing the sizes of the balls.

- Largest ball = nearest
- Medium ball = middle distance
- Smallest ball = farthest away

The differences in size can be exaggerated to make the sense of depth clearer.

---

# Layout Painting Method

## Brush

Use a relatively wide brush for the initial layout.

Avoid:

- pen
- pencil
- very thin-tip drawing tools

---

## Layout Color

Use:

**Light Yellow**

Avoid very dark colors such as:

**Black**

---

## Acrylic Layout Technique

If using acrylic paint:

- Thin the light-yellow pigment with water.
- Keep the initial paint thin and without texture.
- Use clear water to correct mistakes.
- Wet the unwanted area.
- Pat it dry with a paper towel.

Avoid correcting the drawing by covering mistakes with white paint.

---

# Light Source

Before developing shading and texture, determine the light source.

For ART 50 Beginning Painting:

**Use one light source.**

This will help determine:

- highlights
- shadows
- form
- depth

# Part 2 – Color Scheme

ART 50 beginning students are required to use a:

## Monochromatic Color Scheme

This means using variations of **one hue**.

Example:

A blue monochromatic painting may contain different versions of blue that vary in:

- intensity
- value
- lightness
- darkness

The instructor's example uses blue.

---

Continue developing the still life.

## Goals for August 26

Paint the base color onto:

- balls

# Reflection

## What I Learned

I learned that the still-life painting should be planned as one complete
composition. The objects and the background need to develop together
instead of treating the background as something to add at the end.

I also learned how differences in object size can create depth. Larger
objects appear closer, while smaller objects appear farther away.

The monochromatic color approach allows me to focus on value, light,
shape, and composition without needing many different hues.
